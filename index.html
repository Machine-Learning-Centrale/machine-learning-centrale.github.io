Course Description

This course is an introduction to machine learning, with its methods and its mathematical foundations. We study supervised learning: empirical risk minimization, the bias-variance trade-off, classification and logistic regression. We then study decision trees and ensemble methods: random forests, bagging and boosting. The course continues with deep learning (neural networks, backpropagation, stochastic gradient descent) and with unsupervised learning (dimensionality reduction, clustering). Lectures alternate with exercise sessions and hands-on sessions in Python. Students also work in groups on a project with a real dataset.

"Nothing is more practical than a good theory." V. Vapnik

Course material

  • Slides (PDF, version of 17 November 2026). Lecture 1: supervised learning. Lecture 2: logistic regression, evaluation of a classifier, decision trees. Lecture 3: ensemble methods (bagging, random forests, boosting). Lecture 4: deep learning. Lecture 5: unsupervised learning (dimension reduction, k-means).
  • Exercise sheets (PDF, version of 17 November 2026). Sheet 1: classification, cross-validation, linear model. Sheet 2: logistic regression, evaluation of a classifier, decision trees. Sheet 3: mathematical analysis of deep learning.
  • Hands-on sessions (Python notebooks). Ensemble learning: download or open in Colab, with the data set Spambase. Deep learning: download or open in Colab.

Evaluation

  • Projects. Students work in teams of 6 to 8 on a challenge of Challenge Data, chosen freely. The teams are registered on Moodle by Wednesday 17 February 2027. Each team submits at least one solution on the site. Each team presents its work in 15 minutes, followed by 10 minutes of questions, in front of the whole class. The defences take place during the last session, on Wednesday 31 March 2027 (4 hours). The project counts for 50% of the final grade.
  • Final exam. A multiple-choice quiz. Some questions have one correct answer, others have zero, one or several correct answers. Wrong answers give negative points. No computer and no calculator. Handwritten notes are allowed. The quiz counts for 50% of the final grade.
